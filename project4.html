Theory

Game theory is a mathematical framework used to study situations in which the result depends on the decisions of several participants.

Each participant, called a player, chooses a strategy. The combination of strategies determines the outcome and the payoff received by each player.

Game theory is used in economics, computer science, business, politics, and many other fields.

Nash Equilibrium

A Nash equilibrium is a situation in which no player can improve their result by changing their strategy alone while the strategies of the other players remain unchanged.

In other words, every player's strategy is the best response to the strategies chosen by the other players.

Payoff Matrix

Consider a simple two-player game. Each player can choose strategy A or strategy B. The values in the matrix represent the payoffs of Player 1 and Player 2.

A, A → (3, 3)
A, B → (0, 2)
B, A → (2, 0)
B, B → (1, 1)

For example, the result (3, 3) means that both players receive a payoff of 3.

Example of Nash Equilibrium

Suppose both players choose strategy A. The resulting payoff is (3, 3).

If one player changes their strategy while the other player continues to choose A, their payoff becomes lower. Therefore, neither player has an incentive to change their strategy alone.

Nash Equilibrium → (A, A)
